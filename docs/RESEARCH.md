# Contractor discovery and pilot guide

**Status: prepared, not conducted.** No interviews, invitations or messages were sent while building this version.

Interview 5–8 people who personally make pursue/pass decisions at small government contractors. Where possible include an owner, capture/proposal lead and delivery lead, across IT services and another adjacent service category. Ask permission before recording; don't request confidential proposal material.

1. Walk me through the last solicitation you decided to pursue or reject. What first caught your attention?
2. Which requirement took the longest to find or validate? Show the document location if you can.
3. What would make you stop reading immediately: set-aside mismatch, staffing, on-site delivery, past performance, contract economics, or something else?
4. Where have conflicting dates or amendments caused trouble?
5. How much time passes between opening a solicitation and the first bid/no-bid decision? Who else needs to approve it?
6. What tools or spreadsheets do you use today? What have you tried and stopped using?
7. What would make you distrust an AI brief? Would a linked quote resolve that concern, and when would it not?
8. Which document or workflow could you use in a small pilot next week?

Avoid selling features during the discovery portion. Capture concrete events, current time/cost, frequency and existing alternatives. Record evidence separately from interpretation.

For a pilot, ask the reviewer to screen one real, shareable solicitation using their normal process and another with Biddy. Counterbalance order where possible. Observe without telling them where to click. Measure time, missed critical requirements, corrections, confidence and the resulting decision. Explicitly check timezone, amendment precedence, registration timing, named personnel and on-site requirements.

Log participant consent, role, company category, baseline process, observed issue, source passage, correction, severity and follow-up. Recruit domain reviewers from existing professional relationships only when Garv explicitly authorizes that outreach. Treat five observed useful reviews as stronger evidence than a large count of unengaged sign-ups.
